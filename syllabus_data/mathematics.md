# Subject: Mathematics (Middle School - Grade 6-8)

## Unit 1: Algebraic Expressions and Linear Equations
An algebraic expression is a mathematical phrase containing numbers, variables (letters representing unknown quantities), and operations (+, -, *, /).

Key Terms:
- Variable: A symbol (usually x, y, z) that represents an unknown number.
- Coefficient: The numerical factor multiplying a variable (e.g., in 5x, 5 is the coefficient).
- Constant: A fixed numerical value that does not change (e.g., in 5x + 7, 7 is the constant).
- Term: A single number, variable, or product of numbers and variables separated by addition or subtraction.

Solving Linear Equations in One Variable:
The fundamental principle of solving equations is maintaining equality: whatever operation is applied to one side must be applied to the other side.
Steps:
1. Simplify both sides (distribute and combine like terms).
2. Use addition or subtraction property of equality to isolate variable terms on one side.
3. Use multiplication or division property of equality to solve for the variable.
4. Verify by substituting back into original equation.

Example:
Solve: 3x + 12 = 27
Subtract 12 from both sides: 3x = 15
Divide both sides by 3: x = 5
Check: 3(5) + 12 = 15 + 12 = 27 (Valid)

## Unit 2: Ratios, Proportions, and Percentages
- Ratio: A comparison of two quantities by division (a:b or a/b).
- Proportion: An equation stating that two ratios are equal: a/b = c/d (Cross product: a*d = b*c).
- Percentage: A fraction out of 100. P% = P / 100.
