## Unit 1: Photosynthesis and Cellular Respiration
Photosynthesis is the biological process by which autotrophic organisms—primarily green plants, algae, and cyanobacteria—convert light energy from the sun into chemical energy stored in glucose molecules. 

The general chemical equation for oxygenic photosynthesis is:
6CO2 + 6H2O + Light Energy -> C6H12O6 + 6O2
(Carbon dioxide + Water + Light -> Glucose + Oxygen)

Key components:
1. Chloroplasts: The specialized plant organelles where photosynthesis occurs.
2. Chlorophyll: The green pigment inside chloroplast thylakoids that absorbs blue and red wavelengths of sunlight while reflecting green light.
3. Light-Dependent Reactions: Take place in the thylakoid membranes; water is split (photolysis), releasing oxygen gas and generating ATP and NADPH.
4. Calvin Cycle (Light-Independent Reactions): Takes place in the stroma; carbon dioxide is fixed into 3-carbon sugars and ultimately synthesized into glucose using ATP and NADPH.

Importance to Earth's Ecosystem:
Photosynthesis produces virtually all the oxygen in Earth's atmosphere and forms the primary trophic base of nearly all food webs.

Common Misconceptions:
- Misconception: Plants only perform photosynthesis and not cellular respiration.
  Fact: Plants perform respiration 24/7 (consuming oxygen to convert glucose into ATP) and perform photosynthesis during daytime.
- Misconception: Sunlight is absorbed as a chemical reactant.
  Fact: Sunlight is energy driving the reaction, not a chemical reactant.

## Unit 2: The Solar System and Gravity
The Solar System consists of our star, the Sun, and everything bound to it by gravitational attraction: eight planets, dozens of dwarf planets, hundreds of moons, and millions of asteroids and comets.

Planetary Order from Sun:
1. Mercury: Smallest, closest to Sun, extreme temperature swings.
2. Venus: Thick toxic atmosphere, hottest planet due to runaway greenhouse effect.
3. Earth: Only planet known to harbor life, liquid water oceans, protective magnetic field.
4. Mars: The Red Planet, iron-oxide rich dust, thin CO2 atmosphere, polar ice caps.
5. Jupiter: Largest planet, gas giant, Great Red Spot anticyclonic storm.
6. Saturn: Extensive ring system made of ice and rock particles.
7. Uranus: Ice giant, tilted almost 98 degrees on its rotational axis.
8. Neptune: Farthest recognized planet, supersonic winds, deep methane blue.

Gravity:
Gravity is a universal attractive force that acts between any two masses. The force depends directly on the product of their masses and inversely on the square of the distance between them (Newton's Universal Law of Gravitation: F = G * (m1 * m2) / r^2). Gravity keeps planets in elliptical orbits around the Sun and holds moons in orbit around planets.
